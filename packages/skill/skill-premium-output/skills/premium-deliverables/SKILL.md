# Finished deliverables

Use this guide for substantial artifacts beyond websites: documents, presentations, reports, spreadsheets, code packages, diagrams, and media. Scale the work to the user's request. A concise answer may be the complete deliverable; do not manufacture extra files or process.

## Define the output

Identify the audience, decision or task, final format, required content, and how the user will consume or edit it. Respect their supplied template and constraints. Use real source material; distinguish observed facts, estimates, assumptions, and examples. Do not invent citations, metrics, tests, images, or external actions.

For substantial file-based work, save a small valid first version after the necessary source inspection. Include a useful part of the real content, confirm the write succeeded, and check that the artifact opens or executes before expanding it. Add content and styling in completed increments rather than attempting the entire deliverable in one response. Preserve completed files if later work is interrupted, and report unfinished scope when you can respond. Continue to the requested complete artifact; an early saved version does not fulfill the whole brief. A task whose complete output is a short answer needs no extra file.

## Apply the craft appropriate to the medium

- **Documents and reports:** lead with the useful conclusion, organize supporting evidence, use consistent headings and readable typography, and make tables legible. Check pagination, orphan headings, overflow, links, and exported output.
- **Presentations:** give each slide one clear purpose, control information density, align elements, and make charts interpretable at presentation size. Keep sources and caveats near the claims they support.
- **Spreadsheets:** use meaningful labels, consistent units, formulas where appropriate, readable number formats, and explicit assumptions. Check totals, references, error cells, and representative scenarios.
- **Data and research:** trace claims to suitable sources, preserve uncertainty, verify calculations, and choose charts that answer the user's question. A polished chart cannot rescue unsupported data.
- **Code and automation:** deliver a coherent implementation, clear failure behavior, relevant tests, and usable setup. Avoid elaborate scaffolding for a small change. Inspect the actual output and user path.
- **Media and diagrams:** use a coherent composition and visual hierarchy, consistent styling, readable labels, and the intended export dimensions. Keep an editable source when the task calls for it.

Use specialized tools or guides available in the harness. Do not imply that an absent renderer, browser, office application, or image generator exists. Choose a workable supported format and clearly identify any remaining limitation.

## Review and finish

Render or execute the artifact in the form the user will receive. Inspect representative pages, slides, states, or outputs, including edge cases introduced by the change. Fix visible and factual defects. Match the verification to the artifact: a successful save does not establish that formulas calculate, a PDF reads well, or an application works.

For substantial work, make a final pass for content accuracy, hierarchy, consistency, accessibility, and usability. When the deliverable is visually finished (a presentation, a media asset, a diagram, a styled document), dispatch a fresh reviewer — a separate context with no visibility into your private reasoning — the same way the premium-web-experience guide requires for a website: grade rendered pages or exported images against the shared rubric (`../../rubric.schema.json`); for an HTML-rendered artifact, `../premium-web-experience/scripts/review-grade.mjs` also applies. Treat REJECT or REVISE as unfinished work, and if delegation is genuinely unavailable, say so explicitly rather than skipping the step. Keep ownership of the final result rather than forwarding contradictory agent outputs.

Provide the usable artifact and a short handoff: what it contains, what was checked, and any important uncertainty. Link files directly. Preserve the user's originals unless replacement was authorized. Do not describe an artifact as complete, visually reviewed, or tested beyond the evidence you actually obtained.
